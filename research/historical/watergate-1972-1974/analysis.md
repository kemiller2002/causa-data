# Watergate: the break-in and cover-up, 1972-1974

## Scope and source posture

This candidate investigation follows the 17 June 1972 break-in, the first criminal and public explanations, the Senate
investigation and discovery of the recording system, the tape-access dispute, the evidence used to establish a cover-up,
and the resulting judicial, congressional, presidential, and archival consequences. Official records and federal archival
sources are used throughout. Contemporary testimony is not upgraded into a later finding, and a committee finding is not
presented as a criminal conviction.

The FBI, Senate Historical Office, and National Archives chronology are official retrospective summaries. The recording,
Supreme Court opinion, House Judiciary report, grand-jury Road Map, resignation letter, and pardon proclamation are primary
records. The Nixon Library warns that electronic transcripts can differ from the original recordings; W-C012 therefore
paraphrases the archive's description and treats the audio as the primary object.

## Findings established later

- The 23 June 1972 recording is primary evidence that Nixon and Haldeman discussed using a CIA request to stop the FBI's
  money-tracing inquiry (W-C011, W-C012). This later evidence conflicts with the broad August 1972 assurance (W-C026), but
  the comparison does not establish what every White House employee knew at every point.
- The grand jury's Road Map organized 53 evidentiary statements and 97 documents for Congress. It is evidence transmitted
  for an impeachment inquiry, not a verdict (W-C013, W-C014).
- The House Judiciary Committee majority found a course of conduct to obstruct the investigation and listed the alleged
  means. It also adopted abuse-of-power and subpoena-noncompliance articles. Separate, dissenting, and minority views are
  part of the same official report (W-C018 to W-C021).
- The Supreme Court recognized a presumptive confidentiality interest but rejected an absolute privilege against the
  demonstrated, specific need for evidence in a criminal trial (W-C015 to W-C017, W-C025).

## Competing explanations and limits

The investigation preserves three different source postures: the 1972 public denial (W-C003), Dean's 1973 testimony
(W-C006), and the later recording and official findings (W-C012, W-C018). The Judiciary Committee report was not unanimous
(W-C021). Nixon resigned before the full House voted, the Senate held no impeachment trial, and Ford's pardon prevented a
later federal criminal adjudication covered by the pardon (W-C022, W-C023, W-C029).

The 18.5-minute recording gap is established, but these sources do not establish who caused it or why (W-C010). The Road
Map and unindicted-co-conspirator designation are grand-jury actions, not convictions (W-C013, W-C014). These limits remain
explicit unknowns in the dataset.

## Institutional and constitutional consequences

- The House formally authorized an impeachment inquiry; its Judiciary Committee adopted three proposed articles
  (W-C028, W-C018 to W-C020).
- *United States v. Nixon* established the enforceability of the criminal-trial subpoena in this dispute and bounded the
  claimed privilege (W-C016, W-C017, W-C025).
- Nixon resigned on 9 August 1974, and Ford issued Proclamation 4311 on 8 September 1974 (W-C022, W-C023).
- Congress placed the Nixon presidential materials in federal custody and prioritized Watergate and abuse-of-power
  material for preservation and access (W-C024).

## Analytical inference and recommendation

The causal interpretation recorded in the candidate dataset is deliberately narrow: the break-in began the matter, but
the sustained constitutional crisis resulted from efforts to impede investigation and from the resulting conflict over
evidence. That is an analytical synthesis of W-C012 and W-C015 to W-C020, not a quotation or a separate adjudication.

For demonstration, present the chronology with source posture visible: allegation/testimony, primary recording, grand-jury
evidence, committee finding, judicial holding, and unresolved question should remain separate categories (W-C030).
