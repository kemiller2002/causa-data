# Pike Township, Marion County, Indiana: financial audit findings, 2024-2025

## Source package and scope

The original Indiana State Board of Accounts publication is a five-report package:

| Period | Report | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 21370A (PT-S01) | Financial-statement audit and dual accounting-basis opinions |
| 2024 | 21370S (PT-S02) | Supplemental compliance findings and official responses |
| 2025 | 35250A (PT-S03) | Financial-statement audit and dual accounting-basis opinions |
| 2025 | 35250F (PT-S04) | Federal audit, finding 2025-001, and corrective action plan |
| 2025 | 35250S (PT-S05) | Supplemental compliance findings and official responses |

The financial reports cover each calendar year's financial position and operations (PT-C001, PT-C016). In both years,
SBOA issued an adverse GAAP opinion and an unmodified opinion under Indiana's regulatory basis (PT-C002, PT-C017). The
supplemental reports describe procedures performed under Indiana Code 5-11-1, say those procedures were not designed to
find every instance of noncompliance, and direct the reader to the financial report (PT-C003, PT-C018).

SBOA expressly states that official responses were prepared by the auditee and not verified for accuracy (PT-C004,
PT-C018). The dataset therefore keeps auditor findings, written responses, and proposed corrective actions in separate
evidence categories.

## Auditor findings

### Internal controls

SBOA found control deficiencies contributing to noncompliance in both periods (PT-C005, PT-C019). The reports cite
Indiana Code 5-11-1-27(e) and the Uniform Internal Control Standards (PT-C006). The federal finding additionally cites
2 CFR 200.303, 200.1, 200.508, and 200.510(b) and Indiana Code 5-11-1-4(a) (PT-C026).

### Compensation authorization

- 2024: of 18 sampled employees, one received two increases totaling $4,000 without authorization in a union contract,
  salary resolution, or policy; additional review identified multiple employees with similar increases totaling $20,000
  (PT-C007, PT-C008).
- 2025: of 16 sampled employees, one received two increases totaling $4,000 on the same basis; additional review again
  identified multiple employees with similar increases totaling $20,000 (PT-C022, PT-C023).

The reports cite Indiana Code 36-6-6-10(c) and the requirement that compensation be fixed by the township legislative body
and documented in an applicable labor agreement, ordinance, resolution, or schedule (PT-C009).

The report wording does not say clearly whether each $20,000 figure includes or is additional to its sampled $4,000.
The dataset preserves the exact two statements and does not calculate $24,000 (PT-C008, PT-C023).

### Vehicle-use records

For each year, a requested mileage log was not provided and SBOA found no documentation that one had been maintained
(PT-C010, PT-C024). SBOA cites Indiana Code 36-6-4-4 and 36-6-8-3 for authorized official use and the log requirement
(PT-C011). This establishes missing documentation; it does not establish that any trip was personal or otherwise improper.

### Federal reporting

The 2025 Annual Financial Report grant schedule omitted part of Assistance Listing 93.778 reimbursements, understating
the schedule by $1,022,949 (PT-C020). Federal finding 2025-001 classifies the SEFA preparation control as a material
weakness; audit adjustments were accepted and made (PT-C025). SBOA reported no federal award findings or questioned costs
and issued an unmodified major-program compliance opinion for the Medicaid Cluster (PT-C028). The missing amount was a
reporting error, not a finding that money was missing.

## Official responses and corrective actions

The Trustee's responses say the additional-duty compensation practice was stopped pending salary-resolution approval and
future changes would be reviewed; they also say trip logs were placed in each shared vehicle and would be retained and
monitored (PT-C012, PT-C013). The 2025 response attributes the grant omission to coding and identification problems and
describes coordination between fire and accounting staff (PT-C031).

The formal corrective action plan for federal finding 2025-001 assigns communication, reconciliation, and tracking steps,
with immediate implementation and anticipated completion by 31 December 2026 (PT-C029, PT-C030). These are management's
statements and plans, not evidence of operating effectiveness.

The Board responses accept or emphasize the recurring compensation and mileage-log findings and ask about conduct outside
the audit period (PT-C014, PT-C032). They also discuss gift cards and a reimbursement as though they were an SBOA finding,
but no corresponding finding appears in the released reports' contents or Audit Results and Comments (PT-C015). The
dataset records this discrepancy as unresolved and does not manufacture an auditor finding.

## Remaining uncertainties and analytical limits

- Whether the $20,000 figures include the sampled $4,000 (PT-C008, PT-C023).
- Whether the salary-approval and vehicle-log controls described in responses were implemented and effective after the
  audit periods (PT-C012, PT-C013, PT-C034).
- Whether the federal-reporting corrective plan is complete and effective by its anticipated date (PT-C029, PT-C030,
  PT-C034).
- What document or draft caused the Board responses to address a gift-card finding absent from the published reports
  (PT-C015, PT-C032).
- Claims about compensation before 2024 are outside the stated audit periods and remain response assertions (PT-C014).

These are administrative financial and compliance findings. The reports do not establish theft, fraud, criminal conduct,
or intent, and this candidate dataset makes no such inference (PT-C033).
